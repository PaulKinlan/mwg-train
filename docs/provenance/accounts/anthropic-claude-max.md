# Teacher account: Anthropic Claude (Claude Max subscription, Claude Code CLI)

- **Account:** Claude Max consumer subscription. Evidence on this VM:
  `~/.claude/.credentials.json` has `claudeAiOauth.subscriptionType = "max"` and
  `claudeAiOauth.rateLimitTier = "default_claude_max_20x"`. Also reached through pi's
  `claude-code` / `pi-claude-code-provider` providers.
- **Endpoints:** Claude Code CLI, and any Anthropic-hosted model reachable from the subscription.
- **Determination: PROHIBITED as a teacher.** Corpus material generated by this account is
  `A3_teacher_generated` and `excluded_from_training` until Anthropic gives prior authorization in
  writing. This is not a judgement call about competition; the Acceptable Use Policy prohibits the
  activity outright.
- **Captured:** 2026-10-08 (hashes in `../evidence/manifest.jsonl`; quoted line numbers are from the
  text extractions in `../evidence/text/`).

A consumer subscription is governed by the Consumer Terms plus the Usage Policy. The Commercial
Terms are captured for comparison (they govern API/enterprise use and are stricter in one place and
looser in another).

## The decisive clause

Usage Policy, "Do Not Abuse our Platform" list, extracted line 124:

> Utilization of inputs and outputs to train an AI model (e.g., "model scraping" or "model
> distillation") without prior authorization from Anthropic

The Consumer Terms incorporate that policy by reference, extracted lines 26-27:

> You may access and use our Services only in compliance with our Terms, including our Acceptable
> Use Policy, [...]
> You may not access or use, or help another person to access or use, our Services in the following ways:

So the prohibition on training a model on inputs and outputs applies to a Claude Max subscription
exactly as it applies to an API account, and it is **not** limited to competing models in the AUP
wording. "Without prior authorization from Anthropic" is the only route through, and that route is a
negotiated permission, not a public term.

## Secondary clauses that matter

Consumer Terms, extracted line 29:

> To develop any products or services that compete with our Services, including to develop or train
> any artificial intelligence or machine learning algorithms or models or resell the Services.

Read in full, this is a prohibition on developing competing products/services; the "including to
develop or train any ... models" clause is an example of that. It is narrower than the AUP clause,
but it is also the clause a reviewer would point at if the trained artefact were argued to compete
with Claude.

Consumer Terms, extracted line 34:

> Except when you are accessing our Services via an Anthropic API Key or where we otherwise
> explicitly permit it, to access the Services through automated or non-human means, whether through
> a bot, script, or otherwise.

Claude Code is an Anthropic product and is permitted, but the *corpus generation harness* around it
— scripted, automated prompting to produce hundreds of projects — is the pattern this clause
describes. Even setting the AUP aside, automated bulk generation through the subscription is
explicitly conditioned on having an API key or explicit permission.

Commercial Terms, extracted line 18 (contrast only):

> As between the parties and to the extent permitted by applicable law, Anthropic agrees that
> Customer (a) retains all rights to its Inputs, and (b) owns its Outputs. [...] Anthropic may not
> train models on Customer Content from Services.

and line 25:

> D.4. Use Restrictions. Customer may not and must not attempt to (a) access the Services to build a
> competing product or service, including to train competing AI models or resell the Services except
> as expressly approved by Anthropic; [...]

The Commercial Terms are competitor-scoped ("train competing AI models"), while the AUP — which
both plans accept — is not. A reader who only looked at the Commercial Terms would reach the wrong
conclusion for this account.

## Data-use direction (for completeness)

Consumer Terms, extracted line 47: Anthropic may use consumer Materials to train its own models
unless the user opts out of training in account settings, and may use Materials for training even
after an opt-out if the user gives Feedback or the Materials are flagged for a safety review. That
direction (our inputs → their training) is the opposite of what this experiment needs and does not
authorise our use of their outputs.

## Consequence for this experiment

- Teacher arm (`A3_teacher_generated`) from Anthropic: **quarantined**, `excluded_from_training: true`.
- No stored Anthropic-generated corpus material may be re-labelled into `A1`/`A2`; the
  `QUARANTINED_ANCESTOR` check in `src/provenance/record.mjs` blocks a downstream asset whose
  parent chain reaches it.
- If the experiment wants an Anthropic teacher arm, the next action is a written authorization
  request to Anthropic naming the specific use (training a small open-weight web-code model, with
  outputs published as part of a research corpus). Absent that, the arm stays descriptive-only.
