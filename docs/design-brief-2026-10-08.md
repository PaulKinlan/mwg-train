# Research: MWG-applied website training experiment (8 October 2026)

**Evidence status.** This is an experimental-design brief, not a live-verified market or legal survey: this runtime has no web search, fetch, or source-check tool. URLs below are original documentation/terms/pricing pages to inspect before procurement or training. In particular, **all provider-term interpretations, present model availability/licensing, and current GPU prices are UNVERIFIED**; no price or permission is represented as current fact. “Established” denotes a method or a proposition supported by the linked primary documentation, subject to that access limitation; “Recommendation” is this researcher's proposed design, not an MWG or provider claim. The owner's assertion that MWG improves site quality is a motivating hypothesis here, not a verified effect size.

## Summary

Run a small, **rights-cleared, self-generated paired experiment first**: one fixed open-weight code-capable model, ~200–400 original/uplifted projects, a QLoRA or LoRA adapter, and a frozen evaluation on unseen briefs and working browser interactions. The key comparison is not training loss or screenshots alone: under equal inference budgets, does the adapter produce **more correct, secure, accessible, modern, functioning sites** than its unchanged base, without regressions? Keep stronger-model-generated sites in a separate, legally reviewed research arm, not the default training corpus.

## Findings

### 1. Corpus: projects, not just pages

1. **Claim — Recommendation:** Stratify a full *target* of roughly 2,000 generated **projects** (not independent screenshots): commerce/catalogue + checkout simulation (250); informational/marketing/public-service (240); reference/docs/searchable knowledge (200); productivity/dashboard/account (220); community/UGC/forums (170); booking/events/travel (170); media/video/audio (170); learning/assessment (150); games/interactive (130); publishing/news/blog (150); and utilities/forms/identity/account recovery (150). These numbers sum to 2,000, are **planning allocations rather than evidence-based optimum**, and deliberately oversample transactional and form-heavy flows where a pretty static page is inadequate. Independently vary subject matter, audience, copy length, visual density, language/locale, devices, accessibility needs, data shape, and task complexity *within* each archetype. **Support:** researcher recommendation. **Confidence:** medium.

2. **Claim — Recommendation:** Specify one to three executable user journeys per project and require server behavior for an assigned fraction in *every* archetype: SSR or server routes, persistence in an ephemeral SQLite/test database, validation and error responses, search/filter/pagination, authenticated demo sessions where applicable, and simulated checkout/payment or media catalogue APIs rather than real financial credentials. Vary server-rendered and client-heavy architectures; randomize framework and styling families, but lock supported versions and make a runnable contract (`install`, `start`, `test`, seed fixture). An informational site can have a contact-form endpoint; a game can persist scores; reference can serve filtered records. **Researcher inference:** training only from static HTML can teach appearance but offers no positive evidence for server-side behavior. **Support:** design inference. **Confidence:** high.

3. **Claim — Recommendation:** Generate briefs by a factorial template with *held-out combinations*, not by paraphrasing one seed. Allocate distinct design systems and content sources across splits; cap near-duplicate project structures, detect token/code similarity, compare screenshot perceptual similarity cautiously, and use cluster-based splitting by prompt template, scaffold, business domain, rule bundle, and original generator seed. Include low-MWG-quality but otherwise functional originals and already-good originals; do not force a cosmetic diff when no correction is warranted. Preserve train/validation/test manifests and salted content hashes. **Support:** design inference. **Confidence:** high.

4. **Claim — Recommendation:** Keep one record per project with immutable IDs and provenance: brief and all prompt turns; generator/model/version/parameters and usage; consent/license and source lineage; original commit/tree plus dependency lockfile; uplift tool/model/version, MWG guidance snapshot/rule IDs, exact commands and edits; uplifted commit/tree and machine-readable diff; before/after desktop/mobile screenshots; routes/interaction scripts/video or traces; test and browser results; accessibility and security findings; human adjudication; timestamps, locale, archetype, split, near-duplicate cluster, and failure/selection reason. Do **not** publish raw credentials, user data, or copied private content. Keep a manifest linking artifacts by content hash and version; screenshots alone are not a code-generation training example. **Support:** design recommendation. **Confidence:** high.

### 2. Reliable original → uplift pairing

5. **Claim — Recommendation:** Save a reproducible original commit before any uplift. Run baseline install/build/unit/browser flows and capture screenshots and console/network traces. Pin the MWG snapshot and uplift harness; give uplift a narrow patching contract: retain the product brief, routes and content, make justified MWG changes, and explain rule IDs. Save the resulting commit, structured diff, before/after evidence, and an uplift log. An unchanged original is a valid negative/control case but **not** an instructive positive pair. **Support:** design recommendation. **Confidence:** high.

6. **Claim — Recommendation:** Accept a pair only if both versions are runnable and the uplifted version preserves the original task while improving at least one independently confirmed MWG-relevant property. Gates: reproducible build; original and uplifted interactive journeys in a real browser; no new high-severity security issues or broken flows; manual or audited rule-specific confirmation; code/diff attribution (no total rewrite masquerading as a local fix unless deliberately tagged); and licensing/provenance approval. Record rejects, not only winners, so acceptance bias is visible. For a *repair* training set, require original failure and verified post-fix success for the target rule; for a *generation* set, include the original brief and a verified uplifted target. Avoid treating a self-reported uplift rationale as ground truth. **Support:** design recommendation. **Confidence:** high.

7. **Claim — Recommendation:** Score uplift on a fixed vector rather than an opaque “quality” score: intended-rule correctness; functional task pass rate; automated accessibility flags plus manual keyboard/screen-reader spot checks; responsive layout; performance under a pinned test device/network; auth/cookie/form security checks where relevant; visual appropriateness; and edit minimality. Review a random sample of *failures and successes* blind to version; compare automated results on the same test configuration. Explicitly distinguish a rule whose benefit requires browser support from a baseline-compatible fallback; never grant points merely for using a named API. **Support:** design recommendation. **Confidence:** high.

### 3. Training: first run and later scale

8. **Claim — Established tooling, recommendation on fit:** Hugging Face PEFT documents LoRA and quantized-adapter approaches; bitsandbytes documents 4-bit quantization; TRL documents supervised fine-tuning. Full-parameter fine-tuning updates/optimizes all weights and is a poor *first* rental-GPU choice for a 7B–27B model. LoRA trains small adapters on frozen base weights; QLoRA-style training combines quantized base weights with adapters, potentially reducing VRAM at the cost of quantization/runtime complexity. Memory fit depends on context length, batch, optimizer, checkpointing and implementation—**not guaranteed by a model's parameter count**. Start with a 7B–8B QLoRA feasibility run; prefer ordinary LoRA if the rented GPU fits it and throughput is better. **Sources:** [PEFT LoRA](https://huggingface.co/docs/peft/main/en/conceptual_guides/lora), [Transformers bitsandbytes](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/quantization/bitsandbytes), [TRL SFTTrainer](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/sft_trainer). **Support:** documentation plus researcher recommendation; pages not live-fetched. **Confidence:** medium.

9. **Claim — Candidate shortlist, availability/license UNVERIFIED on 8 October 2026:** a code-capable smaller baseline is [Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct); a scale-up comparator is [Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct); alternatives to trial include [Qwen3-8B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B), [Gemma 3 12B IT](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-12b-it), and [Gemma 3 27B IT](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-27b-it). Check each model card for downloadable weights, license/acceptable uses, context, chat template and code-generation capability before selection. “DeepSeek Flash” may mean a hosted API SKU rather than trainable released weights; [DeepSeek models/repository](https://github.com/deepseek-ai) and [API docs](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/) must be checked for the **exact** SKU and weight release. Similarly, do not equate a hosted GLM flash endpoint with an open GLM checkpoint: check [Z.ai models](https://docs.z.ai/guides/overview/models) and [GLM repository](https://github.com/THUDM/GLM-4). For this experiment, exact open-weight checkpoint and commercial-training permissions matter more than a brand name. **Support:** linked model locations as candidate discovery, not verified current catalogue. **Confidence:** medium for shortlist; low for contemporary availability/licensing.

10. **Claim — Recommendation:** First-run SFT examples should resemble the intended inference task: brief → complete runnable project or targeted changes, with explicit paths and file boundaries. If the deployment use is *patching* existing projects, train original code + brief + rule context → verified diff in a separate adapter; if deployment use is *generation from a brief*, train brief → verified uplifted project. Do not train a generation adapter solely on before→after patches and expect it to improve zero-shot generation. Split long projects into coherent, executable components only where cross-file dependencies remain visible; measure truncation rate. Proposed search grid, **not established optimal hyperparameters**: LoRA rank 16/32, alpha 32/64, dropout 0–0.05, attention/projection modules as supported; QLoRA 4-bit NF4 if supported; learning rates 5e-5 to 2e-4, 1–3 epochs, effective batch 16–64 examples, warmup 3–5%, cosine/linear schedule, 4k–16k context as memory permits, gradient checkpointing, 2–3 seeds. Select on held-out task quality, not minimum training loss; freeze training configuration before final test. **Sources:** [PEFT LoRA](https://huggingface.co/docs/peft/main/en/conceptual_guides/lora), [bitsandbytes](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/quantization/bitsandbytes), [TRL SFT](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/sft_trainer). **Support:** documented tooling plus researcher hyperparameter proposal. **Confidence:** medium.

11. **Claim — Recommendation:** MVP: produce ~300 originals, aim for ~200 accepted pairs, reserve ~40–60 *independently created* evaluation briefs beyond the 300 (plus another ~20–30 out-of-family challenges), train one small open-weight base with QLoRA, compare against its frozen base under identical prompts/inference settings, and run a random-label or shuffled-target sanity check on a small subset. Record rejected-pair rate. Full programme is conditional on MVP effect and budget: ~2,000 originals may yield fewer than 2,000 accepted pairs, so do not promise an acceptance count before the pilot. **Support:** experimental-design recommendation. **Confidence:** medium.

### 4. Evaluation: the decision gate

12. **Claim — Recommendation:** Pre-register primary outcome **before seeing test results**: proportion of independent held-out briefs yielding a runnable project that passes its *end-to-end functional task* **and** a prespecified bundle of applicable MWG checks, with no blocker security/accessibility regressions. Publish individual check denominators and applicability; a model must not win by leaving out forms, login, or functionality. Compare base versus adapter on paired briefs with the same generation budget, decoding settings, scaffolds and attempts; test both best-of-one and, if relevant, equal-cost retries. Freeze prompt wording and judge rubric; use a development set for tuning and seal the final test. **Support:** design recommendation. **Confidence:** high.

13. **Claim — Recommendation:** Use three test strata: (A) unseen businesses/topics and phrasings using familiar archetypes/rules; (B) **same style but not identical** prompts with held-out combinations of archetype × MWG rule × framework × locale or task (e.g., accessible booking form with SSR and session handling where training had booking UI and SSR separately); (C) external/novel briefs and already-modern sites, measuring regression and over-application. Partition by template family, site scaffold, topic, and visual/code similarity, not by randomly splitting files or sibling edits. Optionally test a different base model with a separately trained adapter, but do not describe that as transfer without doing the experiment. **Support:** design recommendation. **Confidence:** high.

14. **Claim — Recommendation:** Instrument both automated and human outcomes. Automated: install/build/typecheck; browser click-through journeys with assertions on state and server persistence; form validation and error recovery; keyboard navigation and focus; semantic DOM/label checks; responsive snapshots; cookie/session attributes and CSRF/auth tests where applicable; rule-applicability coverage; duplicate/template similarity; performance under pinned conditions. Human: blinded two-rater assessment of task completion, appropriateness and MWG applicability on a stratified sample, with disagreements adjudicated; a model judge may triage but must not be sole arbiter or see training provenance. Code execution and security testing happen in sandboxed, network-limited containers with no real secrets. Link each measured property to a test artifact; an axe-like automated pass or Lighthouse score is not proof that a site is good or secure. **Sources:** [Playwright testing](https://playwright.dev/docs/intro), [axe-core](https://github.com/dequelabs/axe-core), [OWASP ASVS](https://owasp.org/www-project-application-security-verification-standard/), [Lighthouse](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/overview). **Support:** documented tool locations (not live-inspected) plus evaluation recommendation. **Confidence:** high for design.

15. **Claim — Recommendation:** Report paired pass-rate differences with uncertainty intervals (bootstrap over *prompt families*, not individual screenshots), per-archetype/rule outcomes, seed variability, inference tokens/time and failure taxonomy. Keep distinct a raw base, base + MWG instructions or retrieval at inference, base + deterministic uplift at inference, and trained adapter; equalize total inference/tooling cost in at least one comparison. The relevant question is whether training adds value over simply supplying current guidance or applying the uplift tool. A null is a CI compatible with no useful improvement on the primary endpoint; a negative is a drop in functionality, security/accessibility, generality, or cost-adjusted performance despite a prettier screenshot or higher rule-name frequency. **Researcher inference:** a positive training-set score, self-graded MWG mentions, or cherry-picked screenshots could reflect memorization/judge preference rather than transferable behavior. **Support:** experimental-design inference. **Confidence:** high.

### 5. Teacher → student and terms

16. **Claim — Recommendation:** Separately test mechanisms: (i) self-generated original + deterministic/rights-cleared MWG uplift, (ii) original + uplift generated by an approved teacher, (iii) teacher-authored original + same uplift, and (iv) inference-time MWG guidance without training. This factorial-ish ablation asks whether the gain comes from better source content, actual rule application, or merely strong-model style. Match acceptance quality and total tokens; measure distance to teacher on unseen functionality, not lexical similarity. **Support:** design recommendation. **Confidence:** medium.

17. **Claim — Provider permissions are UNVERIFIED and service/contract-specific:** inspect [Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms) and [Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms); [OpenAI Business Terms](https://openai.com/policies/business-terms/) and [Terms of Use](https://openai.com/policies/terms-of-use/); [Google Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms), applicable [Google Cloud service terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms) and the actual MWG service/employee agreements; [Z.ai terms](https://z.ai/terms) and [DeepSeek terms](https://www.deepseek.com/terms) if those services produce corpus material. **Do not assert a blanket right to distill because a provider says a user owns outputs, or a blanket ban across all plans.** Restrictions may target competitive-model training, reverse engineering, automated extraction, or specific API tiers, and negotiated enterprise terms may supersede public pages. A human legal reviewer should capture the effective version and quote the exact relevant clauses for each account/endpoint and generation date. Until then, teacher-generated originals and teacher-generated uplifted code are **quarantined and excluded from training**, even if transformed. A teacher producing the original and another model applying MWG does *not* necessarily cleanse restricted provenance. **Support:** exact legal positions not verified; named primary URLs are verification targets. **Confidence:** low on current terms; high on quarantine recommendation.

18. **Claim — Recommendation:** Define an approved arm with original projects generated by an appropriately licensed/open-weight model or human-created synthetic briefs, and MWG application via owned rule specifications, tests and rights-cleared implementation. Store a provenance graph per asset and train only on rows with explicit approval. Keep any stronger-provider experimental output in a different storage bucket and split manifest with an `excluded_from_training` flag; evaluate it descriptively only until counsel signs off. MWG guidance itself may have employer ownership/confidentiality constraints: secure authorization for use, storage, model training, distribution and trained-weight publication separately. **Support:** risk-control recommendation, not a legal conclusion. **Confidence:** high.

### 6. Existing-site reproduction

19. **Claim — Recommendation, not legal advice:** Prefer synthetic sites and generic requirements extracted from public patterns, not copied source, assets, branding, copy, APIs, user data or distinctive trade dress. A clean-room procedure has one researcher record abstract functional requirements without passing protected expression to an implementer; the implementer builds with original content/assets and documents independent provenance. A black-box reproduction from screenshots/behavior may still replicate protected expression or violate site terms; avoiding source inspection alone is **not** a legal safe harbor. Do not bypass access controls or scrape authenticated/private material. Check the target site's terms, permission, IP and privacy constraints before collection; `robots.txt` is a crawler protocol, not a copyright license. Label each candidate `synthetic`, `authorized-reproduction`, `clean-room-study`, or `black-box-study`; default both study classes to `excluded_from_training` pending review. **Sources:** [US Copyright Office: What Does Copyright Protect?](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html), [RFC 9309 Robots Exclusion Protocol](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309). **Support:** cautious interpretation plus recommendation; jurisdiction-specific legality unverified. **Confidence:** medium.

### 7. Compute and cost: procurement sheet, not invented prices

20. **Claim — Current hourly rates and availability UNVERIFIED:** request a dated quote from [Runpod GPU pricing](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-pricing), [Lambda GPU cloud](https://lambda.ai/service/gpu-cloud), [Vast.ai marketplace](https://vast.ai/), or [Google Cloud GPU pricing](https://cloud.google.com/compute/gpus-pricing). Each has different instance/interruptibility, GPU memory, storage, bandwidth, region and billing terms; no current numeric price can responsibly be supplied without opening a listing. Record URL, screenshot, date/time, GPU model/VRAM, region, on-demand or spot, disk, bandwidth, tax and minimum charges. **Support:** named pricing-page verification targets only. **Confidence:** low on costs, high on required quote fields.

21. **Claim — Recommendation:** Cheapest *credible* first run is a time-boxed 7B/8B 4-bit adapter feasibility job on **one rented GPU confirmed to fit the measured context and batch**, plus reproducible checkpointing and a small browser-evaluation pool; do a 10–20-example memory/throughput dry run before buying long reservations. Price it as `training GPU-hours × verified hourly rate + evaluation/generation GPU-hours × their rates + CPU browser worker-hours + persistent storage + bandwidth + failed/restarted jobs + provider API tokens`, and include human-review time separately. Obtain token counts and one-epoch throughput from the pilot, then estimate full scale as `observed GPU-hours per accepted pair × target accepted pairs × epochs` adjusted for longer contexts, and `original/uplift generation cost × attempted pairs`, where attempted pairs = desired accepted pairs / observed acceptance rate. Scaling from 200 to 2,000 is **not automatically 10× cost**: generation, failures, longer examples, checks and repeated hyperparameter runs may dominate. A 27B full fine-tune or even adapter run should be quoted only after a VRAM/throughput pilot; do not assume a single 24 GB GPU fits a long-context project. **Sources:** [PEFT LoRA](https://huggingface.co/docs/peft/main/en/conceptual_guides/lora), [Transformers bitsandbytes](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/quantization/bitsandbytes), provider pricing URLs above. **Support:** costing methodology and recommendation; numeric totals deliberately unavailable. **Confidence:** high for method, low for amount.

## Contradictions

- No source-content comparison was possible, so no verified contradiction between provider terms can be reported. Different consumer, business/API and negotiated contracts may differ; their equivalence must **not** be assumed.
- There is a design tension, not a factual contradiction: stronger-model outputs may improve the dataset while creating precisely the provenance/terms problem the owner identified. Treat quality and permission as independent gates.

## Missing evidence

- Exact currently applicable Anthropic/OpenAI/Google/DeepSeek/Z.ai output-use and competitive-training clauses, effective dates, contract scope and employer approval; **UNVERIFIED** at the linked terms pages. Legal review required.
- Current GPU hourly prices, instance availability, storage/egress costs and any provider API generation prices; **UNVERIFIED** at linked pricing pages. Thus no fabricated dollar estimate for MVP or full corpus.
- Exact current model-card availability, license, hardware fit, context/tool behavior and suitability of “DeepSeek Flash” or GLM variants; **UNVERIFIED** at linked model cards/catalogues. Benchmark these rather than assume superiority.
- MWG rule corpus publication/training rights and effect size; the precise uplift harness, test oracle and corpus acceptance yield are not provided. No numerical quality claim is justified yet.

## Risks and first three things to do

1. **Lock provenance and permission:** inventory MWG rules and code rights, choose an open-weight generator/student after checking its exact license, get legal review of the *specific* teacher accounts/terms, and prohibit cross-arm data movement pending sign-off.
2. **Build a 20-project instrumented pilot:** cover at least five archetypes with server journeys; pin versions, record original/uplift commits and browser traces; inspect rejects and refine objective rule checks before scaling.
3. **Freeze the evaluation and price a dry run:** independently author held-out briefs, preregister functional + applicable-MWG primary endpoint and baseline comparisons, then measure VRAM, throughput, acceptance yield and dated rental quotes on ~10–20 examples before committing to ~200 accepted pairs.
